🐊 Bigger Than — the > sign

The crocodile's mouth OPENS towards the bigger number!

Greedy Croc
🐊 Remember: the crocodile is HUNGRY!
The open mouth always points to the bigger number
because the crocodile wants to eat the most!

7 > 3 — Seven is bigger than three

🐊 Smaller Than — the < sign

The mouth still opens to the bigger number — even when it comes second!

Greedy Croc
🐊 The crocodile still wants the big one!
The open mouth still points to the bigger number
This time the bigger number comes second!

2 < 8 — Two is smaller than eight

🏠 Dear Mum and Dad — Please revise this concept!
Your child has been introduced to bigger than (>), smaller than (<), and equals (=).

Please do revision of this concept every day until your child is completely confident. Here are easy ways to practise:

🖐️ Finger game — hold up any number of fingers on each hand. Ask: "Which hand has more?"
🪨 Objects at home — put 4 stones on one side and 7 on the other. Ask: "Is 4 bigger or smaller than 7?"
🃏 Cards — pick two number cards. Let your child draw the correct sign between them.
🐊 Crocodile trick — remind your child: the crocodile is always hungry and always faces the bigger number!

💡 Children often confuse > and <. Keep practising until the signs become second nature!
